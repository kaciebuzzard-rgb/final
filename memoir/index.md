<h1>The Waiting Rooms of My Life</h1>

<p class="memoir-intro">
The Waiting Rooms of My Life is a memoir in progress exploring caregiving,
medical systems, and the invisible labor families carry inside institutions
that were never designed for them.
</p>

<p>
Through personal narrative and reflection, the book traces years spent inside
hospital rooms, school meetings, insurance battles, and the quiet endurance
required to keep moving forward.
</p>

</div>

<h2>Selected Excerpts</h2>


<article class="excerpt">

<h3>The Chair</h3>

<p>
There is a chair in every hospital room.
</p>

<p>
Not the bed where the patient sleeps.
Not the machines that hum and blink through the night.
</p>

<p>
The chair.
The one meant for the person who stays.
</p>

</article>


<article class="excerpt">

<h3>The Hospital Hallway Where Time Collapses</h3>

<p>
Hospital hallways distort time.
</p>

<p>
You can sit for twelve hours and feel like five minutes have passed.
Or walk twenty steps and feel like you’ve crossed an entire lifetime.
</p>

</article>


<article class="excerpt">

<h3>The Invisible Tax of Invisible Labor</h3>

<p>
Caregiving carries a tax no one talks about.
</p>

<p>
It’s paid in phone calls, forms, waiting rooms,
and the constant work of translating between systems
that do not speak to one another.
</p>

</article>
